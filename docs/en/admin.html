Administrator

You run one club: its trainees, classes, schedules, sessions, fees, and the people who work in it.

Read Getting started first — signing in, passwords and switching club.

What you see

Menu What it is for Rights
Dashboard Counts of locations, active classes and this week's sessions, then charts of collected and pending fees, and of cash-flow. read
Locations The places where classes happen. read only
Classes The kinds of training, their price and their default trainers. Search by name… narrows the list, and Active classes only hides the ones you have switched off. full
Schedules Weekly recurring slots that sessions are generated from. full
Sessions The individual occurrences with date, time, trainer and attendance, each carrying a Status of Scheduled, Completed or Cancelled. Above the list, From, Before, Class, Trainer and Location narrow it — each of the last three has an All option to clear it. A Calendar button switches the list to a month, week or day calendar, and Table switches back. full
Trainees The people who train, with guardian contacts and class enrolment. full
Fees Per-trainee charges, with the payments and refunds recorded against them. full
Cards Prepaid visit cards — sale, remaining visits, cancellation with a refund. full
Users The people with access to the club — trainers and customers. full
The chart answers two different questions. Two buttons above it choose which. Billing shows Collected vs Pending fees — for the fees of each month, how much came in and how much is still owed. Cash-flow shows Cash-flow vs Billed — money counted by the day it was paid, against what was charged for that month. The same month can differ between the two, and that is the point: one follows the debt, the other follows the cash. Under the chart, View in fees list opens Fees — with the month already selected when you are looking at a single month, and unfiltered over a longer period.
The chart period. Dashboard shows the last 6 months. The From and To fields set a different period, and either may be left empty — a filled field is cleared with the ✕ at its right edge. From alone runs to the current month; To alone shows the 6 months before it. The period cannot be longer than 120 months (10 years) and the end date cannot be before the start date — in both cases a message appears and the chart stays as it was.

What you can do

  • Create, edit and delete trainees, classes, schedules, sessions and fees.
  • Mark attendance and add a trainee to a session.
  • Put a trainee on the waitlist of a full session, and take them off it.
  • Let customers book, cancel and queue their own trainees on a class — off by default, one switch per class, with a deadline you choose.
  • Generate monthly, per-session or course fees for a whole class at once.
  • Record payments and refunds, and remove one entered by mistake.
  • Sell a prepaid visit card and cancel one with a refund.
  • Add trainers and customers to the club, and remove them from it.
  • Set a new password for someone in your club who cannot receive their invitation — see story 9.
  • Link a trainee to a customer account so a parent can see their information.

What you cannot do, and why

Only a Super Administrator creates locations. You see the list of locations and use it in classes and sessions, but New location and editing a location are not available to you. Opening such an address directly returns you to Locations. Ask your Super Administrator for a new location.
  • You cannot change an existing user's role. Only a Super Administrator does that. When adding a new user you choose between Employee and Customer.
  • You cannot remove a Super Administrator from the user list.
  • You see your own club's data only. There is no view across clubs. If you belong to more than one club, switch club in the top bar and work in each separately.
  • You see the data of your own locations. Every administrator and employee has at least one location assigned; data belonging to locations you are not responsible for is not shown.
  • There are no online payments. PulseDesk charges no bank card. Payments and refunds are entered by hand once the money has moved. (The Cards menu is something else — prepaid visit cards, paid for like any other fee.)
  • You cannot delete a class or a trainee once money is recorded. Delete is refused with This class has fees with recorded payments or refunds. Deactivate it instead, or remove the payments first. — and the same message for a trainee. The deletion would take the fees with it, and the payment history with them. Clear the Active tick in Edit instead: the class or the trainee stops appearing in new work and every past record is kept.

Step by step

1. As an administrator I want to add a new trainee

  1. Open Trainees and press New trainee.
  2. Fill in First name, Last name and Date of birth.
  3. Optionally add Phone, Email and Notes.
  4. Tick the Locations where the trainee trains.
  5. In Classes type at least two letters of the class name, then press the class in the list. It becomes a chip above the field. To remove a chip, press it.
  6. Press Save.
A trainee under 18 needs at least one contact. As soon as the date of birth means the trainee is under 18, a Guardian contacts section opens and says that at least one guardian contact is required. Press + Add contact and fill in First name, Last name, Relationship (Parent, Guardian, Grandparent, Sibling, Other), Phone and Email. Tick Primary contact for the person to call first. Without a contact the record will not save.

For a trainee who already exists, contacts are managed in a separate Guardian contacts section on the edit screen — add and remove them there at any time.

The View button on each row opens the read-only record — handy for a quick look, and the only view your trainers get.

2. As an administrator I want a parent to see their child's information

  1. Make sure the parent has an account in the club with the Customer role. If not, create it first — see story 8.
  2. Open Trainees and press Edit on the child.
  3. In the Guardians section type at least two letters of the parent's name or email address, then press their account in the list. It becomes a chip above the field. Add more than one account if you must — both parents, for example. To remove a chip, press it.
  4. Press Save.

The Linked customer account field is for a different case: when the trainee is an adult and signs in with their own account. One customer account can be linked to one trainee only — reusing it shows That account is already linked to another trainee.

After saving, the parent sees the child in My schedule, My fees and My cards, and can answer session invitations on their behalf.

3. As an administrator I want to create a class

  1. Open Classes and press New class.
  2. Fill in Name and, if useful, Description.
  3. Choose the Billing mode: Per month with Monthly amount per trainee, Per session with Price per session, or Course with Course price, Course start and Course end — one price for the whole run, and the end must be after the start.
  4. Optionally set Capacity (spots) — the number of places in a session. Leave it empty for no limit.
  5. Optionally choose a Waitlist mode for full sessions: None, Automatic (first come) or Email claim — see the attendance story below for what each does.
  6. Optionally tick Customer self-booking. A Booking closes (minutes before start) field then appears — for example 120 to close two hours before the session. Leave it empty and it says Empty = bookable until the session starts.
  7. Choose the Locations where the class runs.
  8. Choose the Trainers — they are used as the default for new sessions of this class.
  9. Press Save.
Changing the billing mode later is allowed. Switching the mode clears the price fields of the old mode, so you enter the new price deliberately. Fees that already exist are not touched — see the fees story for how prices are snapshotted.
What self-booking actually opens. With the tick on, a customer may — for their own trainees, and only for trainees already enrolled in this class — put them on a session, take them off again, and join or leave the waitlist of a full one. The deadline closes all of that at once: after it the buttons are gone and the portal says to contact the club. Everything else stays as it was — customers still cannot move a session, and a booking they make consumes a card visit exactly like one you make. Untick it later and nothing already booked changes — the buttons simply disappear from the portal. The deadline is cleared along with the tick, so set it again if you switch self-booking back on.
Capacity warns, it does not block. The form shows Enrolled 12 / capacity 10, and when enrollment exceeds capacity an amber warning says new sessions will be generated over-full. You may still save — the number is yours to manage.

If there are no locations or employees yet, the form says so plainly — for example No locations available — create one first.

4. As an administrator I want a weekly schedule and sessions from it

  1. Open Schedules and press New schedule.
  2. Choose the Class and the Location.
  3. Choose the Day of week and fill in Start time (HH:MM) and End time (HH:MM) in 24-hour form, for example 18:00 and 19:30.
  4. Press Save. Repeat for each day of the week.
  5. In Generate sessions set From and To — the range you want sessions for. Optionally set Limit to class (optional).
  6. Press Generate sessions.
  7. A result appears, for example Created 24 session(s); skipped 0 duplicate(s). The new sessions are in Sessions.
Generating twice is safe. A session that already exists is skipped and counted as a duplicate. Deleting a schedule does not delete the sessions already generated from it.

5. As an administrator I want to mark attendance

  1. Open Sessions and find the session.
  2. Press Mark attendance on its row.
  3. For each trainee choose a Status: Pending, Present, Absent or Excused.
  4. If somebody turned up who is not on the list, pick them in Add a trainee… and press Add.
  5. Press Save all. The number of saved rows is shown.

The Customer RSVP column shows what the customer answered in the portal: Confirmed, Declined or Wants to reschedule. That is information for you — the real attendance is what you mark. Last marked by shows who marked it most recently. A row the customer booked themselves arrives with Confirmed already set — booking yourself is the clearest answer there is.

Remove from session on a row takes the trainee off the session entirely — use it for a booking made by mistake. If a card visit was used for that booking, the visit returns to the card by itself.

Adding never gets blocked by a card. A trainee whose visit card has no visits left is marked no visits left in the list, and adding them shows a warning that suggests a new card or a fee. The add still works — a full class should never turn a trainee away at the door.
A full session offers the waitlist. When the class has a capacity and the session is full, the add control is replaced by No spots left and — if the class has a waitlist mode — an Add to waitlist control with the queue below it. When a spot frees up (someone is removed), what happens next depends on the class's Waitlist mode: Automatic (first come) books the first trainee in the queue at once and emails the customer — this may consume a card visit without staff action; Email claim emails every queued customer a one-time link, and the first one to open it takes the spot.
A spot freed too late is not filled automatically. Once the class's booking deadline has passed — and on a session that has already started — freeing a spot promotes nobody and sends no claim email. The queue stays as it is and the place is yours to fill with Add a trainee…. Without self-booking the class has no deadline, so this only affects a session that has already begun.

If the class allows Customer self-booking (story 3), the queue can also fill itself: customers add and remove their own trainees from it until the deadline. Those entries are ordinary queue entries — they promote and are emailed exactly like the ones you add.

6. As an administrator I want to charge fees and record a payment

  1. Open Fees.
  2. For classes billed monthly use Generate monthly fees: set the period and press Generate. One fee is created per trainee for every monthly class.
  3. For classes billed per session use Generate per-session fees for a date range.
  4. For classes billed as a course use Generate course fees — one fee per enrolled trainee, with the period and the price taken from the class. A trainee who enrols later gets their fee on the next run; trainees who already have one are skipped as duplicates.
  5. A result appears, for example Created 42 fee(s); skipped 3 duplicate(s). A duplicate means such a fee already exists.
  6. A single fee is created by hand with New fee — pick the trainee, the class, the Amount and the period.
  7. To record a payment, press Edit on the fee's row.
  8. In Record a payment fill in Amount, Paid at, Method (for example "cash" or "bank transfer") and Notes, then save.
The status works itself out. The recorded payments minus the recorded refunds are compared with the fee's Amount: equal amounts give Paid, a smaller sum gives Partial, and nothing paid leaves it Unpaid. That difference is shown on the fee as Net paid, and the Outstanding column shows what is left. The status is never typed in by hand.

Giving money back. The fee's edit screen lists every entry under Payment ledger, each with the Recorded by name, and holds a Refund ledger below it. Record a refund works like recording a payment — amount, date, method, notes — and the status is recomputed the same way. A refund larger than what has actually been paid in is refused, and removing a refund asks for confirmation, exactly like removing a payment.

To settle a fee in full, press Pay the rest next to the Amount field. It fills in the remaining balance; you add the date and save. The status becomes Paid on its own.

You cannot record more money than the fee asks for. A payment above what is left is refused with Payment of 60.00 exceeds the outstanding balance of 40.00 on this fee. For the same reason you cannot lower the Amount below the payments already recorded — that shows Amount 30.00 is below the 40.00 already recorded against this fee. Remove the payment first, then change the amount.

An amount must be more than zero, with two decimal places at most, and not more than 1,000,000. This applies to a fee amount, a payment and a class price.

Removing a payment asks for confirmation and warns that the fee status will be recomputed. Use it when an amount was entered wrongly.

Who still owes for a class in a month. In the fee list choose the Class, choose the Month, and set Status to Outstanding. That shows the unpaid fees and the partly paid ones together, because both still owe money. Change the month to look at an earlier period. Search by trainee name or email… finds one record.

Trainees with no fee. When you choose a class and a month, the trainees enrolled in that class who have no fee for the month are listed above the table. The status filter cannot find them, because they have no record at all. Use Generate monthly fees to create it.
Changing the price of a class. The amount is copied into the fee when the fee is created. If you raise the class price, the fees already created keep the old amount, and the charts stay a true record of what you charged. The new price applies only to fees created after the change. If you already generated fees for a future month, delete them and generate again, or edit each amount.

7. As an administrator I want to sell a visit card

  1. Open Cards and press New card.
  2. Pick the Trainee.
  3. Pick the Scope: one class, or Whole club — a whole-club card is spent on any class's sessions.
  4. Fill in the Number of visits and the Price of the card.
  5. Optionally set Valid until. Left empty, the card never expires.
  6. Press Save.

The sale also creates a fee for the card's price, so the money is collected and reported like any other fee — record the payment there. The card list shows Remaining visits and the State: Active, Used up, Expired or Cancelled.

Visits spend themselves. Adding the trainee to a session takes one visit from their oldest usable card; removing them from the session gives it back. Nobody types visit counts by hand.

Cancelling a card. Press Cancel card on its row. The dialog suggests a Refund amount — price ÷ total visits × remaining — which you may edit, down to 0. The refund is recorded on the card's fee, and the card stops being usable.

8. As an administrator I want to add a trainer or a customer

  1. Open Users and press New user.
  2. Fill in Email. You do not choose a password — the person sets their own.
  3. Optionally fill in First name, Last name and Phone.
  4. Choose the Role: Employee for a trainer, Customer for a trainee or parent.
  5. Choose the Locations the person is responsible for. Press the field to see the list, or type to make it shorter, then press a location. It becomes a chip above the field; press the chip to remove it. For an employee and an administrator at least one location is required — it decides what they can see, so the form will not save without one.
  6. Press Save.
An existing email joins your club instead of creating a second account. If the person already has a PulseDesk account, a message says that the existing account was added to your club and their password is unchanged. One person keeps one account and picks their clubs from the list in the top bar.
The person receives an invitation by email. On save, a link is sent to that address which lets them choose their own password. The link is valid for 48 hours and works once. Until then the account exists but cannot be signed into. Nobody types a password on their behalf, so it stays known only to them — story 9 covers the one case where that is not enough.

If the email does not arrive, the person can request a fresh link themselves with Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen.

9. As an administrator I want to set a password for someone who cannot sign in

  1. Open Users and press Edit on their row.
  2. Fill in New password (leave blank to keep) — at least 8 characters. Left empty it changes nothing, so editing a name or a phone number never touches the password.
  3. Press Save.
  4. Tell the person the password you chose, and ask them to change it themselves.
Saving a password signs that person out everywhere. Every device they were signed in on is logged out at once, deliberately: if an account had to be taken back, sessions left open would defeat the point. They sign in again with the new password.
Prefer the invitation or the reset link whenever you can. A new account gets an invitation, and anybody who forgot their password can ask for their own link with Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen. Both leave the password known only to them. Type one in here when mail is not reaching the person at all — you then know their password, which is the very thing the other two routes avoid.

This works for the trainers, the customers and the other administrators of your club. Somebody's Role is a different matter: only a super administrator changes that, so the field is not shown to you.

10. As an administrator I want to take someone off the club

  1. Open Users.
  2. Press Delete on their row.
  3. Read the confirmation — it asks Remove ivan@example.com from your club? and explains that their account and their memberships in other clubs are unaffected. Only access to your club is removed.
  4. Confirm with Remove from club.

To suspend access instead, open Edit and clear the Active tick — the person stays in the club but cannot sign in.
