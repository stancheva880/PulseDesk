Example setups

Five kinds of business, and how each one is built in PulseDesk — the classes, the prices, the cards, and what each of the four roles does day to day. Find the one closest to yours and copy it.

Read Getting started first, and keep the administrator guide open — the stories below name the screens but do not repeat every field.

  • A reformer pilates studio — packs of trainings, no fixed places.
  • A dance school with halls in two towns — fixed groups paid monthly.
  • A hair and beauty salon — one client at a time, by appointment.
  • A HIIT gym — self-booking and the waiting list, as designed.
  • A children's summer camp — one price for the whole week.

First, the one thing that shapes every setup

A class in PulseDesk is a group with a roster, not an entry in a catalogue. The trainees you put in the Trainees field of a class are that roster, and the roster is what the rest of the app works from.

Two consequences decide how you set up a business. When a session of the class is created — by hand or by Generate sessions — everybody on the roster is put on it. And every one of those bookings takes one visit from that trainee's prepaid card, if they have a usable one.

So there are two honest ways to run bookings, and each setup below picks one:

Way How it works Right for Self-booking
Roster — booked by default You fill the class's Trainees. Everyone is put on every session as it is generated. A customer takes themselves off what they will not attend, and the card visit comes back. Fixed groups: a children's ballet group, a gym membership, a camp group. works
Staff-booked You leave Trainees empty. Staff put each client on the session they actually come to, with Add a trainee… on the attendance screen. That booking takes the card visit. Drop-in: a studio where clients choose their own times, a salon working by appointment. not available
Self-booking is not a shop window. The Book button appears only on classes the club opened for self-booking, and only for trainees already on that class's roster. A customer can never enrol themselves into something new — they can put a trainee back on a session they had cancelled, and join the queue for a full one. That is why a pure drop-in business is run staff-booked.
All amounts are shown in EUR. The currency is fixed for the whole system — there is no setting for it, and no second currency. Every price below is a plain number of euro.

1. A reformer pilates studio

Eight reformers in one room. Clients buy a pack of trainings and come when it suits them; some pay for a single training instead. Nobody holds a fixed weekly place, and a pack is not tied to a month — it is spent when it is spent.

Screen What to set up
Locations One — the studio. It is created together with the club.
Classes One class, for example Reformer pilates. Billing mode = Per session with Price per session set to the single-training price. Capacity (spots) = 8. Waitlist = None. Customer self-booking off. Trainees left empty — this is the roster, and a drop-in studio does not use one.
Schedules One schedule per time slot you open in the week, then Generate sessions for the month ahead. Empty sessions are fine — they are the slots clients book into.
Cards The packs. 10 visits for 250 and 20 visits for 450, each with Scope = the pilates class and Valid until left empty so they never expire. A single paid training is a card of 1 visit at the single price.
Fees Nothing to generate. Every card sale writes its own fee for the price of the pack, and you record the payment there.
Watch out. Generate per-session fees charges every trainee on the roster for every session in the range, whether they came or not — with an empty roster it creates nothing, which is exactly what you want here, so never reach for it. A pack always has a number of visits: “unlimited for 30 days” cannot be expressed, and the nearest thing is a high visit count with Valid until set. And a visit is taken when the client is put on a session, not when they turn up — if they do not come and nobody removes them from the session, that visit is spent.

1.1 Super Administrator — open the studio

  1. Press + next to the club list and fill in the Club name, the Identifier (for example mika-pilates), the First location — the studio itself — and the Administrator email of the owner.
  2. Press Save. The owner receives an invitation by email.
  3. If the studio later opens a second room, add it in Locations with New location. Only you can do that — see the super administrator guide.

1.2 Administrator — one class, two packs

  1. Open Classes, press New class and enter the class exactly as the table above describes. Leave Trainees empty.
  2. Open Schedules and add one schedule per slot you teach — the Day of week with Start time (HH:MM) and End time (HH:MM).
  3. In Generate sessions set From and To for the month ahead and press it. The slots now exist and are empty.
  4. Add each client once in Trainees with New trainee. Do not put anything in the Classes field of the trainee — that is the roster again.
  5. Sell a pack in Cards with New card: the Trainee, Scope = the pilates class, Number of visits 10 or 20, and the Price 250 or 450.
  6. Open the fee the sale created and record the payment in Record a payment. The pack is now paid for and the visits are waiting.
  7. For a client paying for one training, sell a card of 1 visit at the single price. The money then lands in the same place as the packs.
A whole-club pack. If the studio later teaches something else as well and a pack should work for both, choose Whole club as the Scope. A client with both kinds of card spends the class-scoped one first.

1.3 Employee (the trainer) — fill tonight's session

  1. Open Sessions and find tonight's slot.
  2. Press Mark attendance on its row.
  3. For every client who booked by phone or message, pick them in Add a trainee… and press Add. Each add takes one visit from their pack.
  4. A client whose pack is finished shows no visits left. The add still works and a warning suggests a new card — nobody is turned away at the door. Tell the administrator to sell the next pack.
  5. After the class set each row to Present or Absent and press Save all.
  6. Somebody who cancelled: press Remove from session on their row. Their visit goes back to the pack by itself.
Counting the room. The screen shows Occupied 6 of 8, and at eight the add control is replaced by No spots left. With Waitlist = None there is nothing else to offer — the client picks another slot.

1.4 Customer (the client) — the pack in the portal

  1. Sign in and open Cards. My cards shows the pack with Visits remaining, and Never expires under it — the honest answer to “how many do I have left?”.
  2. Open Schedule. My schedule lists the sessions the studio has put them on, with the date, the time and the room.
  3. Answer with Confirmed or Decline so the trainer knows.
  4. Open Fees to see what the pack cost and that it is Paid.
No Book and no Cancel booking button here — that is deliberate. Self-booking is off for this class, so the studio owns the booking. A client who cannot come tells the studio, and the studio removes them from the session, which returns the visit. Decline is an answer, not a cancellation: it does not free the spot and does not give the visit back.

2. A dance school with halls in two towns

One school, two halls in two towns. Children's ballet runs as fixed groups by age, twice a week, paid by the month. The adult evening classes are drop-in and paid from an eight-visit pass. Each town has its own staff, and neither should see the other's paperwork.

Screen What to set up
Locations Two — one hall per town. The first comes with the club; the second is added by the super administrator.
Users One administrator per town, each with only their own hall in Locations. Teachers the same. That single field is what keeps the two towns apart.
Classes — children One per age group and town: Ballet 4–6 (Burgas), Ballet 6–9 (Burgas), Ballet 4–6 (Sofia)… Billing mode = Per month with Monthly amount per trainee. Trainees = the group. This is the roster way.
Classes — adults One per style and town, Billing mode = Per session with Price per session as the single-visit price, Trainees empty. This is the staff-booked way.
Schedules One per day and hall, then Generate sessions per term.
Fees Generate monthly fees once a month for the children's classes.
Cards 8 visits for the adult classes, Valid until about 30 days ahead — a pass that expires, as passes do.

Why one class per town, and not one class in both halls. A class may carry several Locations, but a session belongs to exactly one hall, and the capacity, the price, the teacher and the roster are all different in Burgas and in Sofia. Two classes keep the two groups, their two rosters and their two fee lines honestly apart.

Watch out. Sibling and family discounts are not a feature — Generate monthly fees writes the full Monthly amount per trainee for every child, every month, so you edit the second child's Amount by hand each time (or keep a separate class at the reduced price and enrol second children there). A monthly fee is also not eight visits: Per month charges the month whatever the attendance, so if unused visits must carry over, use a card instead. And an administrator holding one hall cannot see the other town's classes, trainees or class fees — whoever runs both towns needs both halls in Locations. The one exception is a card sold with Scope = Whole club: its fee belongs to no class, so every administrator of the school sees it.

2.1 Super Administrator — the second town

  1. Select the school in the club list in the top bar.
  2. Open Locations, press New location, fill in Name and Address, leave Active ticked and press Save.
  3. Open Users and press New user for the new town's administrator: the Email, Role = Administrator, and in Locations the new hall only.
  4. Check the first town's administrator too. If they should not see the new hall, leave their Locations as it is.
An administrator with no location sees nothing. At least one hall is required, and the form will not save without one. If a colleague reports empty lists everywhere, this is almost always why.

2.2 Administrator — the age groups and the monthly money

  1. Create one class per age group in your hall, as the table above describes, with Capacity (spots) set to what the hall holds.
  2. Add each child in Trainees with New trainee: names, Date of birth, the hall in Locations, and the group in Classes. A child under 18 needs at least one contact: the Guardian contacts section opens by itself, and + Add contact fills it in before you save.
  3. Add the two weekly schedules for the group and press Generate sessions for the term. Every child on the roster is now on every session of it.
  4. At the start of the month open Fees, use Generate monthly fees for the period, and press Generate. One fee per child per monthly class.
  5. For a second child from the same family, press Edit on their fee and set the reduced Amount. Do it again next month — the generator has no memory of the discount.
  6. Record each payment on the fee with Record a payment. The Status works itself out.
  7. For the adult drop-in classes, sell an 8-visit card with a Valid until date and let the teacher add people to sessions as they come.
A free first visit. Sell a card of 1 visit with Price 0. The sale still writes its fee — for 0 — so the trial visit is tracked like any other and the money story stays complete.

2.3 Employee — the Sofia teacher's evening

  1. Sign in and open Sessions. Only Sofia appears, and only the classes where you are a trainer.
  2. Press Mark attendance on the ballet group's session.
  3. The whole group is already listed — they are on the roster. Set each child to Present, Absent or Excused and press Save all.
  4. For an adult drop-in class the list starts empty: add each dancer who turns up with Add a trainee…, which spends a visit from their pass.
  5. A parent asks about the fee: open Fees and read the status. You cannot change it — the administrator takes the money.

2.4 Customer — a parent with a child in each town

  1. Sign in once. My schedule shows both children under their own names, each session with its own hall underneath.
  2. Answer per child and per session with Confirmed, Decline or Reschedule.
  3. Open Fees: one monthly fee per child per group, with Amount, Paid and Outstanding.
  4. Open Cards if the family also bought an adult pass — Visits remaining and the date it is valid until are both there.
One account, one school, both towns. The parent has a single customer account in the school and sees every child linked to it, whichever hall the child dances in. Locations restrict staff, never families.

3. A hair and beauty salon

Two stylists, one client at a time, by appointment, and the price depends on the service. This is the biggest stretch of the five setups: PulseDesk is built for groups, and a salon is a queue of appointments for one. It works, and the box below says exactly where it strains.

Screen What to set up
Locations One — the salon.
Users One Employee account per stylist, with the salon in Locations.
Classes One per service — Haircut, Colour, Manicure. Billing mode = Per session with Price per session = the price of the service. Capacity (spots) = 1. Trainers = the stylists who do it. Trainees empty, Waitlist = None, self-booking off.
Trainees The clients, one record each. Date of birth is required, even for an adult.
Sessions One session per appointment, created with New session: the service as the Class, the date and time, and the stylist as the trainer. No weekly schedule, unless a client has a standing weekly slot.
Fees / Cards New fee per appointment, or a card of 5 visits for a course of cosmetic treatments paid up front.
Watch out. Clients cannot book their own slot: self-booking works only for trainees on a class's roster, and putting a client on the roster of Haircut would book them into every haircut appointment in the diary. Bookings are made by the salon, from the phone or the front desk. There is also no till — no products, no retail, no tips — so anything that is not a service appointment has to be entered as a fee by hand. And nothing checks the diary for you: two stylists working at the same time are simply two sessions at the same time, and no warning appears if you double-book one of them.

3.1 Super Administrator — open the salon

  1. Press + next to the club list, fill in the Club name, the Identifier, the First location — the salon — and the owner's Administrator email.
  2. Press Save.
  3. A second salon later is a second Location, added the same way.

3.2 Administrator — a class per service, then the diary

  1. Create the stylists in Users with Role = Employee and the salon in Locations.
  2. Create one class per service, with Capacity (spots) 1 and the service price in Price per session. Tick the stylists who do it in Trainers.
  3. Add the client in Trainees the first time they call: names, Date of birth, Phone, and the salon in Locations. Leave Classes empty.
  4. Book the appointment: Sessions, New session, the service as the Class, the salon as the Location, the date and the time, and the stylist in Trainers.
  5. Put the client on it: Mark attendance on the session, then Add a trainee… and Add.
  6. Charge it: Fees, New fee, the client, the service class, the Amount and the day as the period. Record the payment when they pay.
  7. For a course of treatments paid up front, sell a card of 5 visits instead. Each appointment you add the client to then spends one visit, and the sale's own fee holds the money.
A standing weekly appointment. A client who comes every Thursday at 10:00 is the one case where a Schedule pays off: add it for that service and hall and generate the sessions. Keep the roster empty and add the client per appointment, as above.

3.3 Employee (the stylist) — the day at the chair

  1. Open Sessions. Only your own appointments appear — the sessions where you are the trainer.
  2. Press Calendar to see the day as a day rather than a list. It is the closest thing to a diary.
  3. Open Mark attendance on an appointment and set the client to Present when they arrive, or Absent when they do not.
  4. A walk-in: pick them in Add a trainee… on a free appointment. A second client on an appointment that already has one is refused with No spots left — the chair is taken.
  5. Press Save all at the end of the day.

3.4 Customer (the client) — their own record

  1. A client who wants to follow their own appointments needs a Customer account, linked to their trainee record in Linked customer account. Most salons will not bother; it is worth it for a treatment course.
  2. My schedule then lists their appointments with the date, the time and the salon.
  3. My fees shows what each visit cost and what is still Outstanding; My cards shows the Visits remaining on the treatment course.
  4. They can answer Confirmed or Decline — a useful signal the day before — but they cannot move or cancel the appointment. That is a phone call.

4. A HIIT gym

Twelve spots a session, three sessions a day, members who train when they can. This is the one setup where every self-service feature is switched on and doing what it was built for: members book and cancel themselves, and a freed spot fills itself from the queue.

Screen What to set up
Classes One class, HIIT. Billing mode = Per session with Price per session as the drop-in price. Capacity (spots) = 12. Customer self-booking ticked, with Booking closes (minutes before start) = 60. Waitlist = Automatic (first come). Trainees = the members. This is the roster way.
Schedules One per daily slot, generated a month at a time.
Cards 10 visits, Scope = the HIIT class. Set Valid until to the end of the period the card is meant to cover — see the box below for why that matters here.
Fees Card sales only. No generator is run.

How a week works. Members are on the roster, so generating the sessions books them all — twelve names on every session before anyone touches a phone. A member who will not come presses Cancel booking and their visit returns to their card. That frees a spot, and the first member in the queue is booked at once and told by email. A member who changes their mind presses Book again, up to an hour before the start.

Watch out. Automatic promotion books somebody and spends a card visit with nobody clicking anything — the class form warns about it, and it is the price of a queue that fills itself. A visit returned by a cancellation is not moved to a later booking that got none: bookings take visits earliest session first, so a 10-visit card put against a month of sessions covers the first ten and the rest are booked with no visit at all. Sell the card for the period it should actually cover. Queueing needs Capacity (spots) — a class with no capacity can never be full, so nobody can ever wait. And after the deadline in Booking closes (minutes before start) nothing self-serves: no booking, no cancelling, and a spot freed then promotes nobody, so fill it yourself with Add a trainee….

4.1 Super Administrator — open the gym

  1. Create the club with +: the Club name, the Identifier, the training floor as the First location, and the owner's Administrator email.
  2. The owner also coaches? They need a second account with Role = Employee on a different email address — only an employee can be a trainer on a class or a session.

4.2 Administrator — one class, self-service on

  1. Create the class as the table above describes. When you tick Customer self-booking, the Booking closes (minutes before start) field appears — put 60 in it.
  2. Choose Automatic (first come) in Waitlist so a freed spot fills itself.
  3. Add the members in Trainees and put the HIIT class in their Classes field. Each member also needs a Customer account, and their trainee record must name it in Linked customer account — without that link there is nobody to book from the portal.
  4. Add one schedule per daily slot and press Generate sessions for the month.
  5. Sell each member a 10-visit card with a Valid until date, and record the payment on the fee the sale created.
  6. Watch the class form's Enrolled 12 / capacity 10 line. More members than spots means new sessions are generated over-full — the app warns and still saves, because the number is yours to manage.
Enrolling everybody books everybody. Adding a member to the class puts them on every future session at once, and takes a card visit for each. On a gym with more members than spots, this is not what you want: keep the roster to the members who really do attend every slot, and let the rest be added per session.

4.3 Employee (the coach) — the door

  1. Open Sessions and press Mark attendance.
  2. The header reads Occupied 12 of 12 on a full session. The add control is replaced by No spots left and an Add to waitlist control with the queue under it.
  3. Somebody does not turn up: press Remove from session. The first member in the queue is booked immediately and their customer gets an email.
  4. Set Present for everyone who trained and Save all.
  5. A walk-in after the deadline: Add a trainee… still works for you. The deadline stops customers, not staff.

4.4 Customer (the member) — a week in the portal

  1. Sign in. My schedule lists this week's sessions, each already booked, because membership means a place.
  2. Travelling on Thursday: press Cancel booking on that session. The spot is freed and the visit returns to the card.
  3. Back after all: press Book — as long as the session is not full and the hour before the start has not begun.
  4. A full session shows Join waitlist. Press it, and the session then shows On the waitlist. When somebody cancels, the booking is made for you and an email says so.
  5. Changed your mind while waiting: press Leave waitlist. That always works, deadline or not — nobody stays stuck in a queue.
  6. Open Cards to see the Visits remaining before buying the next card.
After the deadline the buttons are gone. Within the last hour the card says to contact the club. The coach can still add or remove anybody at the door.

5. A children's summer camp

A day camp: children arrive in the morning, spend the day with their group and its teachers, and go home in the afternoon while their parents are at work. Parents pay once for the whole week. This is what Course billing is for.

Screen What to set up
Locations The camp site.
Classes One per group: Group A (7–9), Group B (10–12). Billing mode = Course, with Course start and Course end on the first and last day and Course price for the whole week. Capacity (spots) = the group size your staffing allows. Trainers = the group's teachers. Trainees = the children. Self-booking off, Waitlist = None.
Schedules Monday to Friday, one slot per day — for example 08:00 to 17:00 — then Generate sessions from the first to the last day of the camp. One session is one camp day.
Trainees One record per child. Every child is under 18, so the Guardian contacts section opens on each one and at least one contact is required — the form will not save without it.
Users A Customer account per parent, named in the child's Guardians field. Both parents may be listed.
Fees Nothing to generate for the enrolled children: putting a child in a course class creates the course fee by itself.
Watch out. The day's activities are not in the app — a camp day is one session, and the plan goes in the class Description or the session Notes. There is no rotation or activity planner, and a half day is a second session rather than a shorter one. Moving a child to another group after the parents have paid leaves the first group's fee behind: the automatic tidy-up only happens before Course start and only while nothing has been paid or refunded, so move the child and then settle the two fees by hand. And Course price is one amount for one run — a second camp week is a second class, with its own dates and its own fee.

5.1 Super Administrator — open the camp

  1. Create the club with +: the Club name, an Identifier such as summer-camp, the site as the First location, and the organiser's Administrator email.
  2. A camp on two sites is two Locations, and each group's sessions belong to one of them.

5.2 Administrator — a group, its week, and the fee that appears by itself

  1. Create the teachers in Users as Employee, and the parents as Customer.
  2. Create the group in Classes with Billing mode = Course, the camp's first and last day in Course start and Course end, and the week's price in Course price. The end must be after the start.
  3. Add each child in Trainees: names, Date of birth, the site in Locations, the group in Classes, at least one Guardian contacts entry, and the parent's account in Guardians.
  4. Check Fees. The course fee for that child already exists, with the camp week as its period and the course price as its Amount — you did not generate it.
  5. Add the daily schedule and press Generate sessions for the camp dates. Every child in the group is now on every camp day.
  6. Record each payment on the course fee. A parent paying in two parts leaves it Partial until the rest arrives.
  7. A late sign-up is the same three steps: add the child to the group, and their course fee appears with them.
The day plan. Put the week's rhythm in the class Description — it is read by everyone who opens the group. Anything special about one day goes in that session's Notes.

5.3 Employee (the teacher) — the morning register

  1. Open Sessions. Today's camp day is there, with your group's whole list already on it.
  2. Press Mark attendance.
  3. As the children arrive set Present. A child who was expected and did not come is Absent, or Excused when the parent told you.
  4. Check the Customer RSVP column before the day starts — a parent who pressed Decline is telling you the child stays home.
  5. Press Save all.
  6. Need a parent's number in a hurry: open the child in Trainees and read the contacts. That is what they are for.

5.4 Customer (the parent) — the week from home

  1. Sign in with the invitation the camp sent, choose a password, and land on My schedule.
  2. Every camp day the child is expected on is listed, with the date, the hours and the site.
  3. The child will miss Wednesday: press Decline on that day so the teacher knows. The place is kept and the fee does not change — one price covers the week.
  4. Open Fees: one fee for the camp week, with the Amount, what has been Paid and what is Outstanding.
  5. Two children in two groups: both appear under their own names, each with their own fee.
Nothing is paid in the portal. PulseDesk takes no bank card. Parents pay the camp the way they agreed, and the camp records it — the portal is where they check that it was recorded.
